    日期和时间

    org-dates

    Timestamps:

    <2026-09-23 Wed>active (shows in the agenda)
    [2026-09-23 Wed]inactive
    <2026-09-23 Wed 10:00-11:30>time range
    <2026-09-23 Wed>--<2026-09-25 Fri>date range
    <2026-09-23 Wed +1w -2d>repeater and warning period
    <prefix>sSchedule (SCHEDULED:). A new date removes CLOSED.
    <prefix>dDeadline (DEADLINE:).
    <prefix>i.Insert an active timestamp (count: include time; count 16: the current time without a prompt). On a timestamp, it changes that timestamp; on a date range, only the end the cursor is on (the prompt starts from it). Repeated right away (no cursor move or edit in between, like Emacs' repeated org-timestamp), it adds -- and a second timestamp: a range. <prefix>i! repeated after <prefix>i. (or the other way round) does the same, the second timestamp taking its own type.
    <prefix>i!Insert an inactive timestamp.
    <C-a> / <C-x>(ctx) Increment / decrement the part under the cursor, like Emacs org-timestamp-change: year, month, day, hour, minute, end time, repeater or warning. The cursor on the character right after a part (a -, : or space) changes that part. On a repeater or warning, the number never goes below 1 (warning: 0) and the unit letter cycles d, w, m, y. Only +N repeaters and -N warnings are changed, like Emacs. A time that doesn't exist locally (skipped by a daylight saving change) becomes the one after the gap, as Emacs's encode-time makes it (02:30 -> 03:30); so do clones and repeaters.
    <S-Up/Down>(ctx) Same as <C-a> / <C-x>. On minutes, step by the second value of time_stamp_rounding_minutes (5) and round to it; a count steps by exactly that many minutes. The end minute of a time range moves by 1. On a bracket, switch the timestamp between active and inactive. With edit_timestamp_down_means_later, <S-Down> is later and <S-Up> earlier.
    <S-Right/Left>(ctx) Day +1 / -1.
    :Org toggle_timestamp_typeSwitch the timestamp at the cursor between active and inactive (in a range, only that end).
    <C-c><C-y>Show the length of the range at the cursor, e.g. "2 days 3 hours 30 minutes"; with a count, insert it after the range as 2d 03:30 ( 2d for dates, 00:20 under a day), replacing an earlier result (org-evaluate-time-range). <d 10:00-12:30> ranges work too (an extension).
    <C-c><C-x><C-t>Toggle the custom display of timestamps (org-toggle-timestamp-overlays): each timestamp is shown as time_stamp_custom_formats (09/25/26 Fri) with conceal and inline virtual text, inside the brackets, like Emacs; a time-range end (-11:30) and a trailing repeater stay visible. display_custom_times = true or #+STARTUP: customtime turn it on; the real text shows in Insert mode (see 'concealcursor'). Timestamps in blocks, fixed-width lines, =verbatim=, ~code~ and #+KEYWORD: lines (except TITLE, SUBTITLE, AUTHOR, EMAIL, DATE) stay as typed, as in Emacs. The formats are Emacs format-time-string formats, the first for dates and the second for dates with a time, e.g. { "%A %d %B %Y", "%A %d %B %Y, %H:%M" }: %a %A %b %B %d %e %m %y %Y %H %I %M %p %j %u %w %U %V %W %G %F %R %T %z %Z ... with the flags - _ 0 ^ # and a width, in English on every system. Brackets around a format are dropped in the buffer and kept in exports. While the display is on in a buffer, exporting it writes its timestamps with the formats (org-timestamp-translate; a format without brackets exports without them).
    <CR>On a timestamp: open the agenda for that day.
    org-calendar

    The calendar

    Date prompts open a floating calendar. It shows the month (with ‹ › around the title) as six weeks with ISO week numbers, so the days of the neighbouring months fill the grid, dimmed. Weekends are tinted, today is underlined and the selected day is highlighted. Below the grid are the selected date in full, its timestamp and its distance from today ("in 3 days", "yesterday"), then the keys. With display_custom_times = true the timestamp is shown in time_stamp_custom_formats (<09/27/26 Sun>, [...] for an inactive timestamp), like the Emacs "=> ..." preview; as in Emacs, a buffer's own toggle or #+STARTUP: customtime doesn't change it. The keys:

    h l j kday -1 / +1, week -1 / +1
    H L < >month -1 / +1
    J K [ ]year -1 / +1
    <C-v> <M-v>three months later / earlier: on today when it is within a month of that month, else on its 1st (Emacs calendar)
    .today
    i ttype a date (see below); a date moved to in the calendar is part of the answer, like Emacs. While typing, the calendar shows the date the answer means ("=> <date>", "(=>F)" when it was taken in the future), like the Emacs overlay (read_date_display_live, default on)
    Tset a time
    !show the agenda of the selected date (Emacs: the diary entries); the windows come back when the prompt ends
    conly in the calendar of :Org goto_calendar: close it and show the agenda from the week of the date (org-calendar-goto-agenda; the key is calendar_to_agenda_key: "default" is c, false none)
    ionly in that calendar, when agenda.diary_entry_file is an Org file: close it and add a diary entry for the date there, as the agenda's i does (calendar_insert_diary_entry_key, org-calendar-insert-diary-entry-key); t still types a date
    x <Del>remove the date (schedule/deadline)
    <CR>select
    <LeftMouse>select the day clicked (<MiddleMouse> too)
    n ponly in a calendar with marked days (such as journal_calendar, org-extensions-journal): the next / previous marked day
    q <Esc>cancel

    With read_date_popup_calendar = false (org-read-date-popup-calendar, or its alias popup_calendar_for_date_prompt) date prompts are a plain "Date+time [2026-09-28]: " prompt instead; an empty answer takes the date in brackets.

    <C-c><Insert the date the calendar was last left on (by a date prompt or :Org goto_calendar), today before that; on a timestamp, change only its date, keeping the time, repeater and warning (org-date-from-calendar).

    A count of 4 (C-u) before the schedule/deadline key removes the date; a count of 16 (C-u C-u) instead asks for a date and sets the warning period (DEADLINE) or delay (SCHEDULED) to the days between it and the entry's date, e.g. <2026-10-10 Sat -5d>. Other counts are ignored, like Emacs.

    The colors are highlight groups linked to standard ones, so they follow the colorscheme; override any of them with nvim_set_hl():

    org-read-date

    Date input (like org-read-date)

    The typed text is read like Emacs org-read-date-analyze:

    ""the default date (the date being changed, or today)
    . +0today
    +3d -2w +1m +4relative to today (unit defaults to days)
    +2htwo hours from now
    ++3d --1wrelative to the default date
    fri +2fri -monnext Friday (a weekday name is never today) / in two weeks / last Monday
    2026-10-01 26-10-01 10-01 10/1 10/1/2026absolute dates
    2026-10-01 Thu +1wextra words (a pasted timestamp) are ignored
    15the 15th, this month or the next
    15.3. 15.3.2027dotted day.month[.year]
    oct sep 15 15 sep 2027 2027month names, a year alone
    w39 2026-w39-5 w39 friISO week (and weekday, default Monday)
    14:00 2pm 15h30 24:00times (combine with any date); 24:00 is the next day
    10:00-11:30 10am-11am 10:00+1:30time ranges
    2026-10-01T10:00ISO date and time
    today tomorrow yesterday now(extensions; Emacs ignores these words)

    Dates out of range roll over (feb 30 is March 2nd). With read_date_prefer_future (default), a day or month without a year is taken in the future: 15 on the 20th is the 15th of next month; "time" also moves a past time of today to tomorrow; false completes the date from the default instead. Before extend_today_until o'clock the default is still yesterday. Text that cannot be read at all is refused with a warning (Emacs silently uses the default date).

    Changes to SCHEDULED and DEADLINE are logged when log_reschedule or log_redeadline is set (or via the #+STARTUP log keywords); setting the same date again is not logged.
